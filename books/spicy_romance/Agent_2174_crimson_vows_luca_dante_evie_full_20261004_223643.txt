### Title
**Crimson Vows**

### Blurb
To his family, he is Luca—the dutiful son molded to inherit an empire built on iron and shadows. To the streets of the North District, he is Dante—a cold-blooded enforcer whose name alone clears the pavement. Luca Dante Borelli answers to no one, until a fractured peace between rival syndicates demands an ancient sacrifice: a blood alliance sealed at the altar.

Evie Caruso never wanted a seat at the table. Running The Velvet Lantern, an upscale speakeasy nestled between neutral territories, was supposed to be her sanctuary away from her family’s violent legacy. But when a brutal syndicate war threatens to incinerate everything she has built, Evie is forced to step into the crosshairs to protect her people.

The terms are absolute. A crimson vow. An unbreakable marriage.

Evie expects a monster; Luca expects a pawn. But inside the walls of his penthouse and behind the velvet drapes of her bar, hostility turns into a volatile, suffocating hunger. As enemies close in from the shadows of the city, Evie discovers the terrifying lengths Luca will go to keep her safe—and Luca realizes that the woman he was forced to claim has become the only thing capable of destroying him.

### Chapter 1

The rain over the North District fell like lead, slicking the cobblestone alleys and turning the neon glow of the city into bleeding smears of crimson and gold. Beneath the street level, down a narrow flight of stone steps flanked by black iron railings, The Velvet Lantern breathed in time with the low purr of jazz.

Evie Caruso wiped down the polished mahogany of the long, curving bar. The air was rich with the scent of roasted vanilla, high-end bourbon, and the faint, coppery tang of the storm leaking in from above. She moved with practiced, deliberate grace, her silk emerald blouse catching the dim glow of the amber sconces. The Lantern was her domain—neutral ground carved out from the carcass of a blood-soaked city, protected by signed treaties and her father’s reluctant indulgence.

Or so she had told herself until forty-eight hours ago, when three Borelli capos were executed outside her rear delivery bay.

The brass bell above the heavy oak door didn’t chime. The heavy hinges simply groaned as the door opened, admitting a draft of freezing autumn air that sent a shiver straight down Evie’s spine. The ambient chatter of low-level fixers, dirty politicians, and district elites died instantly. A suffocating silence rolled through the room like smoke before a wildfire.

Evie didn’t look up immediately. She kept her fingers tight around her linen cloth, her pulse hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird. She knew that tread. Heavy, unhurried, devastatingly certain.

He entered alone, though everyone in the room knew the shadows outside were crawling with his soldiers.

Luca Dante Borelli.

To his father and the inner council who gathered behind bulletproof glass in the Borelli estate, he was Luca—the prodigal heir, sharp-tongued, educated, razor-precise. But down here, in the gutters and backrooms where blood paid the rent, nobody dared breathe the name Luca. Down here, he was Dante. The executioner. The shadow that broke bones and shattered dynasties without ever disturbing the fold of his pocket square.

He shrugged off his heavy wool trench coat, letting it drape carelessly over the back of an empty booth, revealing a bespoke charcoal suit that hugged a frame carved from violence and discipline. Rain glistened in his thick, dark hair. When his eyes swept the lounge—slate gray, glacial, and lethal—the patrons scrambled to look anywhere else. Men swallowed hard into their tumblers; women lowered their lashes in collective, terrified awe.

Then his gaze locked onto Evie.

The temperature behind the bar plummeted, even as heat coiled deep in her belly, illicit and unwanted. He crossed the room with an apex predator’s unhurried swagger, each stride closing the distance between his darkness and her sanctuary.

He pulled up a stool directly across from her, resting his broad forearms on the gleaming wood. A silver signet ring etched with the Borelli crest caught the candlelight. His knuckles were slightly bruised, freshly scabbed.

"Bourbon," his voice was a low, gravelly rasp that vibrated straight through the floorboards. "Neat. Top shelf, *ragazza*."

Evie forced her hands to remain steady as she set the rag aside and reached for a cut-crystal tumbler. She pulled a rare bottle from the back bar, uncorking it with an unbroken rhythm. She poured two fingers, the amber liquid pooling heavily beneath the dim light.

"You're scaring away my paying customers, Dante," she said, her voice smooth, betraying none of the adrenaline flooding her veins.

A razor-thin smirk touched the corner of his chiseled mouth, though his eyes remained entirely humorless. "They shouldn't be here anyway. Not tonight."

"Is that a threat against my establishment?" Evie set the glass before him, deliberately meeting his gaze. "Because the Caruso-Borelli accord states this address is strictly neutral. Your father signed the parchment himself."

Luca reached out, but he didn't touch the glass. Instead, his gloved thumb brushed the edge of the mahogany counter, stopping mere inches from her fingers. The sheer physical presence of him was suffocating; he smelled of petrichor, expensive tobacco, and gun oil. 

"The accord is dead, Evie," he said softly, every syllable landing like an iron nail into a coffin. "Burned along with the four warehouses your uncle's loyalists set fire to at dawn."

Evie froze, her throat constricting. "My uncle doesn't speak for my father. And he certainly doesn't speak for me."

"It doesn't matter who he speaks for," Luca replied. He finally picked up the bourbon, his dark eyes never leaving her face as he tossed the liquor back in a single, smooth motion. The muscular cord of his throat moved as he swallowed. He set the glass down with a soft, final *clink*. "The council met an hour ago. Retaliation was called. The streets are going to run red before sunrise. Your little lounge will be collateral damage within the week."

"I have guards," she countered, defiance flaring hot in her chest, masking the cold dread creeping up
