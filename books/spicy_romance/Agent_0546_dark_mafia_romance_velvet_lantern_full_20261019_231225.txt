### Chapter 1

The air inside The Velvet Lantern always tasted of roasted coriander, smoked bourbon, and old velvet. 

Evie Caruso wiped the mahogany counter with rhythmic, circular strokes, her gaze sweeping over the dimly lit room. Crimson fringe lamps cast low, amber pools over brass-trimmed tables, while the hush of late-night jazz hummed beneath the murmur of clandestine conversations. This place was hers. Every plank of distressed floorboard, every bottle of vintage rye, every inch of soundproofing packed behind the brocade wallpaper had been paid for in blood, sweat, and the absolute refusal to kneel to the syndicates that owned the rest of the city.

Her father had died broke and broken under the boot of the five families. Evie had sworn she would never let a mobster dictate the price of her breath.

"Closing time in twenty, Matteo," Evie murmured to her barback, tucking a stray strand of dark hair behind her ear. 

"Already locked the back cellar, Boss," Matteo replied, checking the heavy deadbolts. 

Before the lock could slide into place on the front door, the brass bell chimed. It wasn't the tentative chime of a lingering patron looking for a nightcap. It was the deliberate, heavy press of a man who didn't knock before he invaded.

The draft that swept through the vestibule was cold, carrying the sharp scent of winter rain, expensive wool, and ozone. 

The room went dead silent. The three remaining couples at the corner booths stiffened, their drinks halting halfway to their mouths before they hastily dropped cash on the tables and slipped past the newcomer without daring to meet his eyes.

Evie didn't run. She stood straight, gripping the linen bar rag in one hand, her heart ticking an erratic beat against her ribs even as her face smoothed into an impassive, hospitable mask.

Two men stepped through the entryway first—thick-shouldered enforcers in tailored charcoal overcoats, hands resting casually near their lapels. But they were merely bookends for the figure who entered behind them.

Luca Dante Borelli.

On the streets, men whispered the name *Dante* when they wanted to summon the devil; he was the reaper of the Borelli syndicate, the clean-up crew, the blade in the dark. Within his father’s gilded brownstone, he was *Luca*, the golden-cufflinked heir apparent whose mind calculated balance sheets and territory lines with terrifying precision.
