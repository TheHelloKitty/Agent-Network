# Crimson Vows

### Blurb
To the ruthless underbelly of the city, he is Dante—an unyielding shadow, a lethal enforcer who leaves only ruin in his wake. To the powerful Borelli syndicate, he is Luca—the calculating heir bound by duty and lineage. 

Evie Caruso wants nothing to do with syndicate wars. All she cares about is keeping The Velvet Lantern, her late father’s legacy bar, alive and out of the crosshairs of bloodthirsty loan sharks. But when her estranged uncle stakes her bar—and her life—in a high-stakes gamble against the Borellis, Luca Dante steps out of the smoke to collect.

The terms are absolute: an arranged marriage to seal a shattered bloodline, or watch the Velvet Lantern burn to ash with her inside it. She signs her soul away to a monster with silver eyes and tailored suits, vowing never to bend to his iron will. Yet beneath the cruelty of his gilded cage lies a devastating, possessive hunger that threatens to consume them both. 

In a world painted in blood and betrayal, their vows will either forge an empire—or burn them alive.

## Chapter 1

Rain lashed against the leaded stained-glass windows of The Velvet Lantern, muting the city’s roar into a low, rhythmic rattle. Inside, the world was steeped in amber, crushed burgundy velvet, and the rich, bittersweet perfume of aged bourbon and lemon peel. 

Evie Caruso wiped down the scarred mahogany bar with practiced, circular motions. It was past two in the morning. The last stragglers had stumbled out into the damp chill of the alley twenty minutes ago, leaving her alone with the hum of the ice machine and the ghosts of her father’s memories.

Her arms ached. Her ribs felt bruised with exhaustion. But every splinter of this place belonged to her—kept alive by sheer stubbornness, sixteen-hour shifts, and a mountain of receipts she kept locked in the iron safe upstairs.

Until the front door chime rang.

Evie didn’t look up immediately. "We’re closed," she called out, tossing the bar towel over her shoulder. "Try the tavern on Fourth if you need a nightcap."

"Turn the lock, Marco," a voice answered. It wasn't loud, but it possessed a terrifying, subterranean gravity that dropped the temperature in the room by ten degrees. "Wait outside."

A heavy deadbolt clicked shut.

Evie’s head snapped up. 

A lone figure moved through the dim fringe of the lounge, stepping past the plush booths toward the illuminated island of the bar. He wore a charcoal wool overcoat, damp at the shoulders from the downpour, tailored with brutal precision over a frame that was all lean, lethal muscle. When the overhead pendant light hit his face, Evie’s breath caught in her throat.

High, aristocratic cheekbones. A sharp jaw dusted with dark stubble. And eyes so pale a grey they looked like polished silver under the bar's warm amber glow.

Luca Dante Borelli.

The streets called him Dante—the enforcer who didn't negotiate, who arrived with silence and left with blood. The syndicate’s high table called him Luca—the heir apparent to the Borelli dynasty, polished in European boardrooms and lethal in subterranean vaults. 

"Mr. Borelli," Evie said, forcing her voice to remain level, though her heart hammered against her ribs like a trapped bird. She kept her hands flat on the mahogany counter, refusing to let them tremble. "The bar is closed. Even for you."

The scent of rain, bitter tobacco, and high-end cologne drifted across the wood between them. It was a cold, predatory smell, like steel dipped in ice water.

"I didn't knock for the spirits, Genevieve," Luca Dante said, his voice a low, gravelly rasp that vibrated straight through her breastbone. He reached up with gloved fingers and slowly unbuttoned his overcoat, slipping it off with fluid grace to reveal the bespoke black vest and crisp white shirt beneath. His forearms were thick with muscle, the cuffs rolled twice, exposing the edge of an intricate black tattoo curling over the cords of his right wrist.

Evie swallowed hard, hating that the slip of her real name from his lips felt like a stroke against bare skin. "My name is Evie. And whatever debts my uncle incurred tonight don't belong to the house."

"Your uncle is an idiot," Luca Dante murmured, sliding into the leather-padded barstool directly across from her. He placed his bare hands on the polished mahogany—the black leather gloves tucked neatly beside them. His fingers were long, blunt-tipped, and ringless save for the heavy silver signet bearing the Borelli crest. "He gambled away three shipping containers of unrefined pharmaceuticals at the docks. Then he tried to pay the difference with deeds he didn't own."

Evie’s stomach plummeted into an abyss. "He told me he had an investor."

"He had a line of credit with my father. Which ran out at midnight." Luca Dante leaned forward slightly, resting his elbows on the counter. The proximity pulled her attention straight to his mouth—sculpted, cruel, yet undeniably sensual. Those silver eyes tracked the erratic pulse fluttering at the base of her throat. "The Borelli family doesn't carry bad paper, Evie. We liquidate it. Or we collect the collateral."

"The Velvet Lantern isn't his collateral," she shot back, stepping back half an inch, trying to summon the iron in her spine. "The deed is in my name. My father gave it to me before he died. Enzo only runs the floor."

"Enzo put your signature on the promissory note."

The room seemed to tilt. A cold wave washed over her skin. "He forged it."

"Undoubtedly," Luca Dante agreed without missing a beat. His gaze never wavered from her face, drinking in the fury and the sudden dread burning in her dark eyes. "Which makes him a thief on top of a degenerate. My men are currently holding him in a warehouse three blocks south, waiting for my signal to drop him into the harbor."

Evie’s fingers gripped the lip of the bar. She hated Enzo—hated his gambling, his loud mouth, his stench of cheap gin and entitlement. But he was her mother’s only living brother. "You can't just kill him."

"I can," Luca Dante said simply. "I’m Dante on these docks. Killing a thieving parasite isn't a chore; it's basic maintenance." He tilted his head, the amber light catching the sharp angle of his jaw. "Or I can be Luca. And Luca is willing to negotiate an alternative settlement."
