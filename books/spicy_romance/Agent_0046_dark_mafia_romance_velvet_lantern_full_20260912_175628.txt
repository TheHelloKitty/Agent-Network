### Chapter 1: Pouring for the Devil

The ice melted too fast in the heat of The Velvet Lantern.

Evie Caruso slid a polished crystal tumbler across the zinc surface of the bar, catching the condensation on a cocktail napkin before it could stain the dark wood trim. Jazz curled through the haze of cigarette smoke and expensive cologne—a slow, sultry saxophone that sounded more like an ache than a song. The speakeasy was buried beneath the concrete bones of Chicago’s old district, an empire of plush burgundy velvet, low-watt amber sconces, and dangerous men with heavy jackets.

Men who carried pistols tucked against their ribs and knives slipped into their boots.

Evie kept her spine straight, her chin lifted. Working here was a dance along the edge of an open grave, but it paid enough to keep the loan sharks away from her brother’s door for a few more days. Or so she prayed.

"Another rye, doll," a man grunted down the line, slurring his words as he tapped dirty knuckles on the rail.

"Coming right up," Evie said, her voice smooth, neutral, revealing nothing. She turned to the tiered glass shelves, reaching for the bottle of Maryland rye. Her black silk blouse shifted against her shoulder blades, catching the dim glow of the chandelier overhead.

Then, the air in the room died.

The shift wasn't loud. There was no shout, no crash of broken glass. It was a sudden, freezing drop in atmospheric pressure. The murmur of whispered deals and illicit laughter shrank into reverent silence.

Chapter 2: The Scent of Gunpowder and Gold

The heavy oak door at the top of the stairs had opened, admitting the cold breath of the city along with the man who owned it.

Evie didn’t turn around right away. Survival behind the rail of The Velvet Lantern depended on knowing when to look and when to become invisible. But her skin prickled, the fine hairs on her arms standing on end. Her fingers tightened around the neck of the rye bottle as the heavy tread of polished leather shoes descended the staircase, rhythmic and unhurried. 

Every patron in the room shrank into their leather booths. Conversation ceased entirely. 

To the old-money syndicates and the family elders, he was Luca. The prodigal son, the crown prince of a dynasty built on bloodlines and bruised knuckles. But down here, where blood pooled on damp concrete and iron ruled the night, the streets called him Dante. A demon whispered in dark alleys, a name spoken only when men looked over their shoulders.

Luca Dante Borelli had entered the room.

Luca Dante Borelli walked in like the devil himself had decided to inspect his personal precinct. 

He didn’t take the back stairs. He cut straight across the floor of The Velvet Lantern, his bespoke charcoal wool coat swinging open to reveal the tailored line of his vest and the dark, deadly promise of his broad shoulders. On the street, men swallowed their tongues and called him Dante—a moniker earned in blood, broken glass, and silence. To his blood, he was Luca. But standing behind the polished mahogany bar, polishing the exact same heavy crystal tumbler until her knuckles went stark white, Evie saw every ruthless facet of him at once. 

His dark eyes found her immediately. He didn't scan the room. He didn't check the corners. He honed straight into her, his gaze heavy, possessive, and thick with an unspoken reckoning that sent a shiver straight down the seam of her spine.

The low hum of jazz from the vintage speakers seemed to warp, swallowed by the sheer gravity of his entrance. Two of his lieutenants flanked him, but Luca waved them back with a single, sharp flick of his wrist. They melted into the shadows near the private booths without a word.

Luca didn't stop until he reached the bar, settling his tall frame directly across from her. Up close, the scent of him flooded her senses—cool autumn rain, cedarwood, and the faint, bitter metallic tang of gunpowder. 

"Evie," he murmured, his voice a low, gravelly scrape that vibrated in the soles of her shoes. 

"Mr. Borelli." She set the glass down with measured precision, refusing to let him see the tremors threatening her fingers. "You’re late for your private room. Your uncle’s men have been waiting an hour."

A ghost of a smirk touched the sharp cut of his lips, dangerous and razor-thin. "Let them wait. Salvatore knows who holds the leash in this city. And it isn't him."

Luca leaned forward, planting large, calloused palms against the slick mahogany. The sleeve of his tailored shirt pulled tight over corded forearms, inked with dark, intricate lines that disappeared up toward his shoulders. He didn't blink. He held her hostage with nothing more than the pitch-black intensity of his stare. 

"You didn't answer my call this morning, little songbird."

Evie swallowed hard, keeping her chin lifted. "I work nights, Dante. You know that. I was sleeping."

"Don't lie to me." The temperature dropped an inch. His tone wasn't raised, but it carried the weight of an iron vault swinging shut. "I know you were at St. Jude’s by seven. Handing over an envelope to Father Frances. Paying off debts that don't belong to you."

The breath seized in her throat. She gripped the edge of the sink beneath the counter, feeling the cold stainless steel bite into her skin. "My family's business is my own."

"Your father’s sins became Borelli business the night he forged my father’s seal on a shipment of Albanian steel," Luca said quietly, tilting his head. He reached out slowly, giving her every chance to retreat, but Evie remained rooted to the floorboards. 

His leather-gloved knuckles brushed the soft curve of her jawline. The contrast was agonizing—the cold, smooth calfskin against the fever-warm flush of her skin. Her pulse hammered wildly against the hollow of her throat, right beneath his index finger.

"And everything that touches Borelli business," he whispered, leaning closer until his breath ghosted across her parted lips, "belongs to me. Including you, Evie."

A dark, electric jolt arced between them. Her stomach flipped, hot and hollow. It was maddening how terrifying he was, yet how violently her body responded to his proximity. Every instinct screamed for her to run, to flee out through the Lantern’s alley doors into the freezing city streets. But her feet wouldn't move. She wanted the heat radiating from him; she hated how much she wanted it.

"I’m not a piece of collateral," she managed, her voice dropping to a fierce whisper that only he could catch over the low saxophone playing in the background.

"You're whatever I decide you are," he countered softly, his chest rising and falling in deep, controlled measure. "And right now, you're the only reason your brother still has all ten of his fingers."
