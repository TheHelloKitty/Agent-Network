# CRIMSON VOWS: BLOOD AND VELVET

### BLURB
To the North End syndicates, he is Dante: a ruthless enforcer carved from cold marble and calculated violence. To the Borelli bloodline, he is Luca: the heir who shoulders the sins of the family empire. When Evie Caruso’s father gambles away his loyalty and vanishes into the Chicago night, he leaves behind an unpaid ledger and a singular collateral: the deed to The Velvet Lantern, an opulent speakeasy drenched in smoke, amber liquor, and dangerous secrets.

Evie refuses to abandon the bar her mother built, even when Dante steps out of the shadows to claim ownership. But Luca doesn't just want the territory; he wants the fierce, golden-eyed woman behind the mahogany counter. What begins as a lethal debt negotiation twists into an intoxicating game of possession and dark obsession. In a world where blood seals every contract, Evie must choose whether to burn down The Velvet Lantern to escape him—or yield to the beast whose touch awakens every forbidden desire she swore to bury.

    *   *Role:* Evie’s estranged, indebted father; former accountant for the syndicate whose reckless thievery ignites the conflict.

CHAPTER 1



### Chapter 1: The Blood on the Ledger

The Velvet Lantern breathed in smoke, amber bourbon, and the low, sultry drawl of a muted brass trumpet. Tucked beneath the cracked ribs of the North Quarter, it was an oasis of velvet banquettes, polished brass, and shadow—the only sanctuary Evie Caruso had left in a city devoured by wolves.

Evie wiped the ring of condensation from the dark mahogany counter with deliberate, steady strokes. Her sleeves were rolled to her forearms, her tailored black vest hugging her waist, but beneath the practiced composure, her nerves hummed like live wires. 

It was half past midnight on a rain-drenched Thursday, and the air had gone sour. The patrons—dockworkers with bruised knuckles, low-level bookmakers, and neighborhood drunks—had cleared out forty minutes earlier, spooked by nothing more than an intuition. A shift in the wind that always heralded violence.

The bell above the brass-trimmed door remained silent, but the heavy oak swung open on oiled hinges anyway.

Two men entered first: broad-shouldered silhouettes in tailored Italian wool, rain gleaming on the brims of their charcoal fedoras. They flanked the entryway, hands tucked neatly inside their coat pockets, scanning the corners with dead, methodical stares. Evie didn't blink. She knew the breed. Syndicate enforcers. Borelli soldiers.

Then came the man who commanded the storm.

He stepped over the threshold, unbuttoning his charcoal cashmere overcoat with slow, predatory grace. The street knew him by a single, whispered name that carried the weight of an executioner’s axe: *Dante*. But the blood that ran thick through the city’s veins belonged to the Borelli dynasty, where he sat at the right hand of the Don as Luca Dante Borelli.

He was massive—broad-framed, towering past six-foot-three, his presence swallowing the oxygen out of the lounge. His dark hair was brushed back, damp with rain, emphasizing high, aristocratic cheekbones, a sharp, unyielding jawline shadowed with stubble, and eyes the color of chilled obsidian. Cruel. Beautiful. Lethal.

Evie tightened her grip on her bar towel under the counter, keeping her chin lifted and her spine rigid. Her mother hadn't bled for this floorboards-and-liquor sanctuary just for Evie to cower before a mafia prince.

Luca shed his coat, tossing it carelessly onto an empty corner booth, and approached the bar. His footsteps made no sound against the Persian runner. He wore a midnight-blue three-piece suit without a tie, the collar of his white silk shirt unbuttoned to reveal the hollow of his throat and the faint edge of a black ink tattoo creeping up his collarbone.

He stopped directly across from her. Up close, he smelled of cedarwood, expensive tobacco, and the metallic tang of fresh rain.

"Evie Caruso," he said. His voice was low, gravelly, and dragged over gravel and silk. It made the small hairs on the back of her neck stand on end.

"Mr. Borelli," she countered, her tone cool, refusing him the deference of his street moniker. "We closed twenty minutes ago. The taps are shut."

A faint, mocking smirk touched the corner of his chiseled mouth. "The city sleeps when I say it sleeps, *tesoro*."

"The city might. The Velvet Lantern has set operating hours."

One of his men by the door made a subtle movement, but Luca raised a single gloved finger, and the guard froze. Luca leaned forward, resting his large, calloused palms flat against the mahogany.
