## Chapter 1: Blood and Bourbon

The lock on the alley door gave way with a soft, mechanical click that sent a cold prickle down Evie Caruso’s spine. 

It was two in the morning. Last call had chimed an hour ago, the last lingering patrons of The Velvet Lantern ushered out into the drizzling autumn chill of Manhattan. The jazz playlist had run dry, leaving only the hum of the vintage brass chandeliers and the dull drone of the street-level ventilation.

Evie didn’t turn around immediately. She kept polishing the cut-crystal tumbler in her hands, watching the back corridor in the tarnished mirror behind the liquor tiers. 

A shadow filled the frame. Broad-shouldered, impossibly tall, cutting through the amber gloom of the speakeasy like a drawn blade. 

"Bar's closed," Evie said, her voice smooth, low, and entirely devoid of the tremor vibrating in her ribs. "And delivery entrance is for people who bleed when you shoot them. You look like you might just rust."

"Clever," a deep baritone rumbled from the threshold. The voice was gravel wrapped in silk, resonant enough to vibrate through the floorboards. "Your uncle always said you had sharp edges, Evie."

At the mention of her uncle, Evie set the glass down with a deliberate tap. She turned slowly, resting her palms against the dark mahogany bar. 

The man stepping into the pool of amber light wasn't a stranger to anyone who read the obituaries or walked through Little Italy after midnight. 

Luca Dante Borelli. 

His family called him Luca when they wanted mercy, though rumors claimed he rarely gave it. The rest of the five boroughs simply whispered the name *Dante* when someone vanished into the Hudson. 

He wore a charcoal bespoke three-piece suit beneath a heavy black wool overcoat, damp with rain. Dark hair swept back from a face carved out of ruthless Roman angles—high cheekbones, a sharp, unyielding jawline shadowed with evening stubble, and eyes the color of smoke over an open fire. He pulled off black leather gloves finger by finger, tucking them into his breast pocket.

"You're trespassing, Mr. Borelli," Evie said steadily, meeting that suffocating gaze.

"Dante to people who owe me," he replied, taking three long, unhurried strides toward the bar. Every movement had the predatory economy of a panther stalking through tall grass. "Luca to family." He stopped on the other

The brass bell above the front door seemed entirely irrelevant against the sudden dead silence of the room. Evie didn’t flinch. Her heart hammered against her ribs, hard and fast, but her fingers remained still where they gripped the edge of the counter.

"My uncle ran this place into the ground on his own dime," Evie said, her voice smooth, betraying none of the adrenaline singing through her veins. "The deed transferred to me three days after they buried him. His gambling debts didn't come with the liquor license."

"Everything comes with the name Caruso in this neighborhood," Dante murmured. His gaze traced the line of her throat down to the modest scoop of her emerald-green silk blouse, lingering just long enough to make the heat rise beneath her skin. "Antonio borrowed from my father. Then he borrowed from me. He signed over the Lantern as collateral six months ago, right around the time he started betting on fixed fights that didn't stay fixed."

Dante reached into his inner pocket. For a split second, Evie’s breath caught, her hand twitching toward the snub-nosed .38 tucked beneath the cash drawer. But he didn't pull out steel. 

He slid a folded parchment across the counter, smoothing it flat with long, scarred fingers. The gold signet ring on his pinky flashed under the dim amber sconces. 

Evie looked down. Antonio’s messy, trembling scrawl was unmistakable at the bottom of the promissory note, stamped with the Borelli family crest in black wax.

"He had no right," she whispered, anger flaring through her shock. "This place was my grandfather’s. It’s all I have left."

"Then you should have watched him closer," Dante said softly. He stepped around the corner of the bar, moving into her space with absolute, terrifying calm. He didn't draw a weapon, didn't raise his voice, but the sheer physical presence of him filled the narrow walkway between the bottles and the taps. 

Evie held her ground, refusing to retreat an inch, even when his towering frame cast a shadow that swallowed her whole. Up close, the flecks of gold in his smoky eyes were distinct, dangerous, and startlingly vivid. 

"I don't leave debts uncollected, Evie," he said, his voice dropping into that low, velvet register that set every nerve ending on fire. "If I let Antonio’s niece walk away with four hundred grand of Borelli money, the families in Brooklyn think I’ve gone soft. And I assure you, nothing about me is soft."

"So kill me," she challenged, lifting her chin, defiance blazing in her dark eyes. "Put a bullet in my head and take the keys. That’s what you do, isn't it? Dante the executioner."

A slow, dark smirk curved the corner of his lips. It transformed his severe, aristocratic features into something wicked. 

He lifted one hand. Evie braced herself, expecting violence, but his calloused fingers merely caught a loose strand of her dark hair, tucking it gently behind her ear.
