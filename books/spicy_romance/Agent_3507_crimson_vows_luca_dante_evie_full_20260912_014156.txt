### Title: Crimson Vows

### Blurb
To the ruthless syndicates controlling the city’s concrete veins, he is Dante—a cold, calculating enforcer whose name alone silences rivals. To the inner circle of the Borelli empire, he is Luca—the dutiful heir carrying the weight of a blood-soaked legacy. He lives in shadows, answers to no one, and leaves no room for mercy.

Until Evie Caruso.

Fierce, unyielding, and fiercely protective of her legacy, Evie runs The Velvet Lantern, an upscale speakeasy nestled between neutral territory and enemy crosshairs. When her late father’s debts threaten to incinerate everything she has built, the Borelli family steps in with a non-negotiable lifeline: an arranged marriage to solidify fractured borders. 

Evie agrees to the crimson vows to save her sanctuary, expecting a loveless cage with a monster. Instead, she finds a man torn between two identities, matching her fire with a dark, consuming hunger. Behind closed doors, their animosity turns to scorching obsession. But as rival factions circle The Velvet Lantern to ignite a war, Luca Dante must decide if he can tear down the city to keep his wife safe—and Evie must risk trusting the deadliest predator in it.

### Chapter 1

Rain streaked the leaded-glass transom of The Velvet Lantern, blurring the neon glow of the street into smears of bruised violet and amber. Inside, the world was built of polished mahogany, velvet upholstery the color of dried blood, and the low, seductive hum of a saxophone drifting from the corner stage. 

Evie Caruso stood behind the marble-topped bar, running a clean linen cloth over a heavy crystal tumbler. Her hands were steady, even if her pulse had been erratic for three days straight. 

"Last call was ten minutes ago, Miss Caruso," Marcus murmured from the end of the bar. He was wiping down the brass taps, his heavy shoulders tight beneath his vest. Marcus had been her father’s enforcer back when the Caruso name carried weight. Now, he was her head bartender and unofficial bodyguard, watching the shadows with the weary eyes of a man who knew an executioner was overdue.

"I know," Evie said quietly, setting the glass on the shelf alongside rows of rare bourbons. "Lock the front doors, Marcus. Clear out the lingerers in the back booths."

"He’s coming tonight, isn’t he?"

Evie didn't flinch, though a cold shiver traced the line of her spine beneath her black silk dress. "He said midnight. It’s twelve-fifteen."

"We could run. Take the cash in the floor safe. Cross state lines."

"Run where?" Evie met Marcus’s eyes, her chin lifting. "My father died leaving a four-million-dollar crater with the Borelli syndicate. You don't outrun that kind of blood money, Marcus. You either settle it, or you wind up tied to a cinder block at the bottom of the docks. This bar is everything my mother and I have left. I’m not abandoning it."

Before Marcus could answer, the brass bell above the front entrance chimed. 

A sharp draft swept in, carrying the scent of ozone, damp wool, and cold leather. The two remaining patrons in a far booth glanced toward the entrance, took one look at the figure framed in the doorway, tossed down a crumpled stack of bills, and practically scrambled through the rear

exit into the alleyway.

The door clicked shut, the deadbolt snapping into place under the gloved hand of the man standing there.

Luca Dante Borelli filled the threshold with the quiet, suffocating gravity of a storm front. To the family, behind the iron gates of their estate on the north shore, he was Luca—the prodigal son who had mastered the ledger books and inherited his grandfather’s ruthless clarity. But out here, along the asphalt arteries of the lower wards, no one dared use that name. To the streets, he was Dante. A specter in a bespoke charcoal overcoat, cut wide across the shoulders and long enough to conceal whatever steel rested at his ribs.

His dark hair was slicked back against the rain, sharp cheekbones catching the amber amber-glow of the Velvet Lantern’s vintage sconces. He pulled off his leather gloves slowly, finger by finger, his eyes never leaving Evie. They were a piercing, obsidian shade—cold, deliberate, and entirely devoid of mercy.

"You're late," Evie said, forcing her voice into a register of cool, unshakable poise. She smoothed her hands over the mahogany surface of the bar, feeling the worn grain anchor her trembling pulse.

"Rain slowed traffic on the bridge," Dante replied smoothly. His voice was a low, resonant rumble that seemed to reverberate in the hollow of her collarbone. "Besides, I wanted to give your regulars time to scurry. I prefer privacy when I conduct audits."

Marcus shifted on his heels, his massive hands dropping toward the edge of the counter where an unregistered Colt was taped beneath the lip. 

Dante didn't even turn his head. "Touch that piece, Marcus, and my associates outside will burn this brick foundation to cinders with you inside it. Go home to your sister. This doesn't concern you."

Marcus hesitated, his jaw working, eyes flashing between Dante and Evie.

"Go, Marcus," Evie ordered softly, not breaking eye contact with the syndicate heir. "It's alright. Lock the service door behind you."

"Evie—"

"I said go."

With a ragged exhale, the older man threw his rag down and retreated through the swinging pantry doors. The heavy latch clicked shut. 

Silence fell over the Velvet Lantern, thick and suffocating, broken only by the muted patter of rain against the stained-glass transom.

Dante stepped forward. His gait was fluid, predatory, the measured pace of a man who owned every room he entered simply by walking through the door. He unbuttoned his coat, letting it drape over the back of a barstool, revealing a tailored black suit that molded to a broad, heavily muscled frame. A crimson pocket square offered the only slash of color against the dark wool.

He took the center stool, resting his forearms on the polished wood. Up close, the faint scent of cedarwood, expensive tobacco, and clean rain rolled off him, invading her senses.

"You poured the 1982 Macallan earlier," Dante observed, his gaze flicking to the amber stain lingering in the crystal tumbler on the service mat. "Anticipating bad news, Miss Caruso?"

"Anticipating an aggressive collector," Evie countered, reaching beneath the counter to pull down a clean lowball glass. She poured two fingers of the scotch and slid it across the wood until it stopped an inch from his hands. "Four million is an impossible figure, Borelli. My father was a degenerate gambler at the end, but your books are padded."
