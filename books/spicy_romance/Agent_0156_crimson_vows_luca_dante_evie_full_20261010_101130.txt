**TITLE:** Crimson Vows: Bound in Blood and Velvet

**BLURB:**
In the cutthroat underworld of the Borelli syndicate, survival demands absolute obedience. Evie Caruso thought she had escaped that dark legacy, pouring drinks and scrubbing mahogany as the sole owner of The Velvet Lantern, a sanctuary tucked deep into the city's neon-drenched shadows. But when an old debt comes due, the past walks through her brass-trimmed doors in the form of a waking nightmare: Luca Dante Borelli.

To his ruthless family, he is Luca—the dutiful heir and quiet strategist. To the asphalt, the docks, and the terrified rival crews, he is Dante—the executioner with ice in his veins. 

Sent to claim Evie’s bar as collateral, Luca finds himself ensnared instead. When rival factions threaten her life, Luca offers an ancient, unforgiving solution: a blood-bound contract. A crimson vow made before family and enemies alike. To keep her safe, she must wear his ring. To keep his crown, he must take her into his bed. What begins as a lethal transaction ignites into an unyielding, possessive fire that neither can control.

***

**CHARACTERS:**

*   **Luca Dante Borelli:** The dual-faced heir of the Borelli crime family. Called "Luca" by his inner circle and blood relatives, and whispered as "Dante" by the streets who fear his ruthless efficiency. Cold, calculating, and relentlessly protective once his possessive instincts are triggered.
*   **Evie Caruso:** The fiercely independent owner of The Velvet Lantern. Daughter of a disgraced syndicate accountant, she fought tooth and nail to build an honest haven amidst a crooked city. Sharp-witted, resilient, and utterly unimpressed by mob bravado.
*   **Matteo Borelli:** Luca's cousin and ruthless enforcer, eager to take territory by force rather than strategy.
*   **Clara Russo:** Evie’s trusted head bartender, sharp-eyed and protective of Evie's peace.
*   **Don Salvatore Borelli:** The aging patriarch whose declining health sets off the vicious territory war.

CHAPTER 1



The last notes of Billie Holiday had long faded into the amber-stained shadows of The Velvet Lantern. 

Evie Caruso wiped down the hand-carved mahogany bar, her fingers tracing a deep groove near the brass rail where her father’s signet ring used to tap out nervous rhythms. It was two in the morning. Outside, the North End rain hammered against the frosted glass windows, washing away the city’s filth but doing nothing to clean the salt-and-whiskey scent baked into the century-old brick walls.

The Lantern was hers. Every floorboard that groaned, every bottle of vintage rye tucked into the high shelves, every speck of dust dancing in the dim amber pendants. Her father, Marco, had died six months

ago, leaving her with an iron safe of receipts, an empty checking account, and a ledger soaked in crimson red ink. 

The bell above the heavy oak door chimed. 

A sharp draft sliced through the warmth of the room, smelling of ozone, wet pavement, and expensive Italian wool. Evie’s rag stopped midway across the bar. Her pulse kicked against the hollow of her throat. 

The lock had turned at midnight. No one walked into The Velvet Lantern at two without a key—or the kind of arrogance that treated deadbolts like suggestions.

Footsteps sounded on the floorboards, slow and deliberate, measured with the certainty of a man who owned the air he breathed. Out of the shadows by the vestibule stepped a figure broad enough to swallow the light. 

Luca Dante Borelli.

To his mother, to the gilded drawing rooms of the North Shore, he was Luca—the heir, the polished prince of the Borelli syndicate. But out here, where the blood ran into the harbor and the alleyways swallowed screams, he was Dante. The executioner. The shadow behind every closed door.

He shook the rain from his dark hair, sending small droplets scattering across the dark wood floor. His tailored charcoal overcoat was damp at the shoulders, clinging to a frame forged in violence. Beneath the cut of the fabric lay sculpted muscle, broad shoulders tapering to a lean waist, radiating a heat that immediately seemed to swallow the room’s damp chill. 

Dark, predatory eyes settled on her, heavy with an intensity that made Evie’s breath hitch in her chest. 

"We're closed," Evie said, forcing her voice to stay even, though her knuckles turned white against the damp cotton cloth. 

Dante didn’t stop. He crossed the room with an easy, fluid grace, rounding the nearest leather booth and stopping right at the edge of the mahogany rail. Up close, the sheer size of him was suffocating. He smelled of rain, cedarwood, and the faint, bitter metallic tang of gunpowder. A sharp jaw, dusted with dark stubble, framed lips carved into a cold, assessing line. 

"The front latch gives too easily, Evie," he said, his voice a low, gravelly baritone that vibrated right through the soles of her shoes. "A flick of a blade and you're exposed."

"I was doing just fine before you broke in."

"I didn't break in." He reached out, his large, scarred hand resting on the bar top. His fingers drummed once, twice—right against the groove her father had worn down over forty years. "I came to collect."

Evie felt the blood drain from her face, only to rush back as hot, defensive fury. "The debt was Marco’s. My father is in the ground."

"Debts don't get buried with the bones," Dante countered softly, stepping in closer. The space between them shrank until she could see the flecks of gold in the dark irises of his eyes, the slight crooked break in his nose that kept him from being pretty and made him terrifyingly handsome instead. "Marco took three hundred thousand out of the Borelli treasury to float his poker habit. The Don gave him thirty days. The Don died. Marco died. But the money didn't disappear."

"I don't have three hundred thousand dollars, Dante." Her voice cracked slightly, betraying the terror she fought so hard to bury. "You can strip the brass off the walls. You can take the liquor. It won't cover a tenth of it."

Dante leaned forward, placing both palms flat on the mahogany, trapping her behind the bar with the sheer cage of his presence. His dark eyes flicked over her—from the loose curls of chestnut

Dante didn't miss the twitch of her fingers. His gaze tracked down to her wrist, then back to her eyes, dark and knowing.

"I might. But not tonight." 

He pulled out a heavy cream envelope, tossing it onto the polished mahogany. It slid across the wood and stopped an inch from her knuckles. Sealed with thick, crimson wax, stamped with the Borelli crest—a coiled viper beneath a jagged crown.

"What is this?" she demanded, refusing to touch it.

"Your father’s ledger was messy, but his promises were very clear," Dante said. He began to unbutton his overcoat, shrugging it off with fluid grace and draping it over the back of a barstool. Beneath it, his crisp black dress shirt strained against the broad expanse of his shoulders, the sleeves rolled to mid-forearm, displaying the intricate black ink tracing the tendons of his arms—vines, thorns, and names she didn't want to decipher. "Marco didn't just borrow against the Velvet Lantern. He borrowed against his word as a made man's son. And when the liquidity ran dry, he signed an addendum."

Evie felt a sickening chill ripple down her spine. "An addendum."

"Open it."

Her fingers trembled despite her best efforts as she broke the wax seal. The parchment inside was thick, the legal boilerplate unmistakable, but the ink at the bottom was faded blue ballpoint—her father’s frantic, scrawling signature. 

Her eyes scanned the lines. *Collateral... defaulted balance... transfer of custodial title... binding marital union.*

The breath stalled in her lungs. She stared at the words until they blurred into black static.

"No," she whispered.

"Yes."

"This is barbaric. It's not the middle ages, Dante! You can't enforce a—a contract like this! The law—"

"The law?" Dante let out a low, humorless laugh that sent shivers skittering over her skin. He stepped around the corner of the bar, moving into her territory. Evie backed away, but there was nowhere to go; the liquor shelves pressed hard against her back, glass bottles clinking softly as she bumped the shelf.
