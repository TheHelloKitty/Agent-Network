# CRIMSON VOWS

### Blurb

To his family, he is Luca—the dutiful, sharp-edged heir bound to restore honor to a fractured empire. To the ruthless criminal underworld of New York, he is Dante—an unyielding enforcer whose mercy is a myth. 

Evie Caruso knows only one thing about the syndicate: stay in the shadows and keep your debts clean. Working behind the polished mahogany of The Velvet Lantern, she survives on sharp wit and invisible boundaries. But when an explosive secret within her family’s ledger drags her into Dante’s crosshairs, survival demands a brutal compromise. 

To prevent an all-out turf war, blood must be met with blood—and vows must be sealed in crimson.

Forced into an arranged union to secure a fragile peace, Evie vows never to surrender her pride to the monster the city fears. Yet behind the gilded gates of the Borelli estate, the lines blur between the predator on the streets and the fiercely protective man who watches her every breath. In a deadly game of territorial conquest and fractured loyalties, the greatest threat to Evie’s survival isn't Dante’s enemies—it’s the scorching, inevitable fire that sparks every time he pulls her against his chest.

## Chapter 1

Rain streaked the leaded-glass windows of The Velvet Lantern, turning the streetlights of lower Manhattan into smeared halos of amber and blood. 

Evie Caruso wiped down the dark mahogany of the bar with a methodical, practiced rhythm. The rag caught a stray drop of bourbon near the brass rail, polishing the wood until it gleamed like liquid obsidian under the low-hanging art deco lamps. Around her, the room hummed with the muted drone of late-night drinkers, jazz whispering from hidden speakers, and the low clink of crystal. 

The Lantern was an upscale sanctuary—or as close to one

as this city permitted. Men in three-thousand-dollar suits came here to forget the blood on their cuffs, and women draped in silk came to pretend they didn't know how it got there. Evie knew better. She kept her chin down, her dark eyes observant, and her tongue behind her teeth. Survival in this town wasn't about strength; it was about invisibility.

The heavy oak door swung open, dragging in a gust of damp river air and an immediate, suffocating silence.

The jazz didn't cut out, but the room’s pulse did. Glasses stopped halfway to parted lips. Whispers died in throats.

Evie felt the shift before she looked up. The fine hairs on the back of her neck lifted, an instinctual tremor warning her that an apex predator had crossed the threshold. 

He stepped out of the rain like a specter carved from charcoal and granite. A tailored charcoal overcoat clung to broad, imposing shoulders, the fabric beaded with water. Beneath it, his midnight suit was immaculate, sharp enough to cut. Dark hair was pushed back from a face of aristocratic cruelty—high, blade-like cheekbones, a square jaw shadowed with dark stubble, and eyes that held the chilling stillness of a frozen lake.

Luca Dante Borelli.

To the aging capos who toasted him at private family dinners, he was Luca, the favored son and ruthless future of the Borelli empire. But to the streets, to the men who bled out in alleyways and the rivals who prayed for mercy in the dark, he was only Dante. Death in a silk

Evie felt that stare like a blade tracing the exposed hollow of her throat. Every survival instinct she’d honed since her father’s fall screamed at her to duck behind the counter, to run through the kitchen and out into the rain-slicked alley. But running from a Borelli only made you prey. 

Slowly, deliberately, she set the glass down on the wooden rack. The crystal clinked against the shelf, the tiny sound unnaturally loud.

Dante took long, measured strides toward her. The floorboards didn’t creak beneath his polished oxfords. Up close, the lethal perfection of his face was overwhelming—harsh, aristocratic cheekbones, a strong jaw shadowed with dark stubble, and eyes the color of bruised obsidian. A faint, jagged scar cleaved his left eyebrow, a cruel reminder of how he’d earned the right to run the north docks before he was thirty.

He stopped at the bar, directly across from where she stood. 

The scent of him washed over her: expensive bergamot, rain, and the faint, bitter tang of gun oil. It was dizzying. Intoxicating. Danger dressed in silk and wool.

"Evie," he murmured. 

His voice was a low, velvet scrape against her nerves, sliding down her spine and pooling low in her stomach. It wasn’t a question; it was an arrival.

Evie gripped the damp linen rag under the counter, keeping her chin lifted. She would not shake. She had buried her name, her bloodline, and her pride to survive in this city. She wouldn't let him see the rabbit kicking against her ribs.

"Mr. Borelli," she replied, her voice steady despite the dry ache in her throat. "The Lantern closes in fifteen minutes. I can pour you a scotch, but you’ll have to drink it fast."

One corner of his sculpted mouth twitched, a humorless curve that made him look purely predatory. He placed one large, calloused hand flat on the lacquered wood between them. The gold signet ring of the Borelli syndicate gleamed under the amber pendant lights.

"I didn't come for the rotgut you keep behind this counter, little Caruso."

A jolt went through her at the name. *Caruso.* 

She hadn't heard it spoken out loud in three years. Hearing it from him felt like having the breath knocked straight out of her lungs. 

"I think you have me confused with someone else," she whispered, her voice tightening. "My name is—"

"Genevieve Carla Caruso," he cut in smoothly, leaning forward until only a breath of space separated them across the bar. His gaze dropped to her mouth, lingering on her parted lips for a heavy, agonizing second before dragging back to her eyes. "Daughter of Marco Caruso. The girl who vanished into thin air the night the Caruso house burned to the ground."

She wouldn't crumble now. Not before a Borelli.

He laid it flat on the mahogany counter, resting two long, scarred fingers over it. "The night the fire took your estate, Marco Caruso came to my father. Evie stared at the wax seal. Her stomach plummeted into a cold, bottomless pit. "My father died in that fire."

"He did," Luca agreed, his tone chillingly even. "Because he was ten minutes too late making the call. But before the line went dead, the deal was struck. Blood for blood. An alliance sealed by the only collateral Marco had left to offer."

"No," Evie whispered, stepping back from the bar. Her hips bumped against the lower shelving of liquor bottles, setting the glass clinking like a discordant chorus. 

Luca took the paper and slid it across the wood until it tapped against the edge on her side. "A blood vow, Evie. My father gave his word, and a Borelli never leaves a debt unpaid. You were promised to me."

"I am not a piece of land to be traded!" she snapped, anger finally breaking through the ice of her fear. Her cheeks flushed hot. "Look at me, Borelli. Look around you. The Carusos are dust. I pour whiskey to men who can barely count their change. I have no dowry. I have no soldiers. I have nothing to give you."
