# Crimson Vows

### Blurb
To the ruthless syndicates carving up the city’s shadows, he is Dante—an unyielding phantom whose word is law and whose blade never hesitates. To the Borelli bloodline, he is Luca—the dutiful heir saddled with an empire built on old blood and heavy sins. He moves through the underworld untouched, cold, and entirely untouchable. 

Until he walks through the rain-slicked doors of The Velvet Lantern.

## Chapter 1

Rain lashed against the leaded glass of The Velvet Lantern like crushed diamonds, blurring the streetlamps of the North Ward into smears of amber and rust. Inside, the world was steeped in heavy velvet, burnished mahogany, and the slow, sultry murmur of a brass saxophone looping through the low-lit air.

Evie Caruso wiped down the polished bar top, her knuckles aching with familiar exhaustion. The Lantern was full, but quiet in the way upscale hideaways were supposed to be—subdued conversations, the clink of crystal tumblers, the faint drift of tobacco smoke clinging to the high ceiling. It was her haven. The only clean thing her father had left behind before he choked on his own bad decisions six feet underground.

Or so she had told herself until three days ago, when a red wax-sealed envelope had slid under the brass-trimmed front door with a number that made her pulse stutter in her throat.

"Another rye, Evie," murmured Marco, one of her regulars at the end of the counter.

"Coming up," she said, keeping her voice smooth as cream. She turned to pull the bottle, letting muscle memory take over. 

Then the front door opened.

A draft of freezing October rain swept across the threshold, cutting through the scent of bourbon and aged cedar. The low hum of chatter in the lounge didn’t just falter; it died in an instant, snuffed out like a pinched wick.

Evie froze, the heavy glass bottle poised over a tumbler. 

A man stepped into the entrance. He shrugged off a tailored charcoal wool coat dripping with rain, passing it without a glance to the hulking shadow lingering behind him on the street. Underneath, his black suit was cut with an immaculate, razor-sharp precision that made the room look instantly cheap. Broad-shouldered, impossibly tall, and moving with the silent, coiled balance of a wolf prowling familiar territory, he stepped down into the sunken barroom.

CHAPTER 3



Dante stepped fully into the pool of amber light cast by the hanging brass lamps above the bar. Rain clung to the heavy wool of his tailored charcoal coat, the scent of damp wool and ozone cutting through the sweet, stale perfume of bourbon and cigar smoke that permanently inhabited The Velvet Lantern.

He didn't move like a patron. He moved like an inevitability—silent, broad, predatory.

"A reaper carries a scythe, *piccola*," he said, his voice a low, gravelly rasp that scraped directly along her exposed nerves. "I prefer using my hands."

He reached out and slid onto the middle stool, his long legs parting to bracket the wooden base. With deliberate ease, he unbuttoned his coat, letting it fall open to reveal the holster strapped tight against his broad chest, the matte black handle of his Kimber gleaming against the stark white of his dress shirt. He didn't draw the weapon. He didn't need to. The lethal weight of his frame was threat enough.

Evie didn't drop her gaze. To look away was an admission of weakness, and in this city, weakness was an invitation for wolves to feed. "You didn't answer my question."

"I was thirsty," he said mildly, leaning forward. His forearms, corded with dark ink that coiled past his rolled-up cuffs, settled onto the polished mahogany counter. "And your door was unlocked. Careless for a Caruso girl, especially tonight."

A cold wire tightened in Evie's stomach. *Tonight.* She had heard the whispers through the distributor network—the gunfire down near the docks, the sudden silence from her uncle’s operation on 4th Street. 

"I can take care of myself," she said, reaching for a bottle of rye with hands she willed into absolute obedience. She set a heavy tumbler before him and poured two fingers of amber liquid. "Drink. Then get out."

Luca Dante looked down at the amber pool, then lifted his eyes. They were a piercing, ruthless green, ringed with dark lashes that gave him the deceptive look of a fallen angel. He didn't touch the glass. Instead, he reached across the narrow divide of the bar and captured her wrist.

The contact was an ignition. 

Evie gasped softly, the sound catching in her teeth. His fingers were broad, calloused, and blistering hot against the pale, sensitive flesh of her pulse point. He didn't squeeze hard enough to bruise, but the grip was absolute iron. Her heart fluttered beneath his thumb like a trapped sparrow, giving away every ounce of terror and the suffocating, shameful heat pooling between her thighs.

"You're lying to yourself, Evie," he murmured, his thumb stroking slowly over the frantic jump of her vein. The touch was agonizingly intimate, a dark caress wrapped in a death warrant. "Your uncle ran. Left the city three hours ago with Borelli money in his duffel bag."

Evie froze, the breath trapped in her lungs. "Enzo... wouldn't."
