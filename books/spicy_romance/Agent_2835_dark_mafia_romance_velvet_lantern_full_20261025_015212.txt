### Title: Crimson Vows: Blood & Velvet

### Blurb
To the ruthless underworld of Chicago, he is Dante—a ghost with an iron fist, the deadliest enforcer the Borelli syndicate has ever fielded. To his blood, he is Luca, the calculating heir destined to take the crown. Luca walks through violence as if it were rain, untouchable and unbothered. Until he steps into The Velvet Lantern.

Evie Caruso knows the golden rule of surviving in the shadows: keep your eyes down, your accounts balanced, and never owe a mobster a favor. Running The Velvet Lantern, an upscale neutral-ground speakeasy, is her life’s work and her sanctuary. But when her late uncle’s hidden ledger surfaces—painting a target on her back from every rival family in the city—her sanctuary turns into an execution chamber.

When Luca claims her bar and her life under Borelli protection, Evie refuses to be another piece on his chessboard. Yet beneath Dante’s cold, lethal exterior lies a hunger that matches her own defiant fire. In a world bound by blood and treachery, surrender isn't an option—unless it's in the dark, behind locked doors, where the predator becomes her protector, and every vow is written in crimson.

CHAPTER 1



*Luca.*

To the bloodhound scavengers prowling the alleys of Chicago, to the made men whose knees trembled when footsteps echoed in a warehouse at midnight, he was Dante. A reaper in a tailored charcoal suit, wearing leather gloves stained with sins that holy water couldn't wash clean. 

He didn't make bargains. He didn't offer mercy. He collected debts in flesh, lead, and silence.

And tonight, Dante’s debt led him straight down a nondescript flight of stone stairs off 22nd Street, into the belly of the beast.

The brass sign outside was understated, almost invisible beneath the soot and rain: *The Velvet Lantern.*

Behind the heavy mahogany door, the city’s cacophony dissolved into a low, smoky thrum of tenor saxophone, the faint clink of crystal, and the scent of aged bourbon layered with vanilla and expensive perfume. Dim amber sconces cast long, decadent shadows against exposed brick and blood-red velvet draperies. 

It was neutral ground. A place where Capos from the north side could nurse an Old Fashioned three stools down from south-side lieutenants without pulling hollow points from their shoulder holsters.

Or so they pretended. 

Luca Dante Borelli pushed through the heavy velvet curtain framing the entryway. Rainwater glistened on the broad expanse of his shoulders, clinging to his dark wool coat like beads of quicksilver. His sharp, cut-glass jaw was shadowed by dark stubble; his eyes, the color of cold river slate, cut through the haze of cigar smoke and settled on the center of the room.

More specifically, on the woman behind the polished oak bar.

Evie Caruso.

She moved with an effortless, predatory grace that made his blood simmer low in his gut. Her dark curls were pinned back in an artfully disheveled crown, exposing the elegant, pale curve of a slender throat. She wore a deep emerald silk blouse that dipped just low enough to hint at the swell of her breasts, tucked neatly into high-waisted black trousers that hugged hips built to ruin a man's composure. 

She was wiping down the surface with a white bar towel, her gaze flicking across the room with a vigilance sharper than any bouncer’s. 

Luca didn't move toward her immediately. He lingered in the shadows near the corner archway, letting the chill of the outside air bleed off his skin while he watched her. He knew everything about her. He knew her uncle, Enzo Caruso, had died three days ago in an alleged hit-and-run that smelled like Marcone gasoline from five miles away. He knew Enzo had kept a quiet, dirty little black ledger hidden somewhere in these very walls. 

And he knew that every syndicate between Lake Michigan and the state line was coming to tear this velvet haven apart to find it.

Evie felt the shift in the air before she saw him. Her spine straightened, tension coiling along her shoulders. She set down the highball glass in her hand, her amber eyes scanning the room until they collided with his.

She didn't flinch. She didn't drop her gaze.
