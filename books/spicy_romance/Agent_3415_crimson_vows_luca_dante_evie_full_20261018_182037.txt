To his family, he is Luca—the dutiful firstborn heir to the Borelli syndicate. To the cold, rain-slicked streets of the underworld, he is Dante—the merciless shadow who settles debts with lead and silence. He is a predator trapped between the expectations of an empire and the savage instincts that keep him alive.

Then there is Evie Caruso. 

Daughter of a ruined bloodline, Evie is fiercely protective of what little she has left, scraping together an existence behind the amber glow and worn mahogany of The Velvet Lantern. But when her father’s reckless gambling leaves the Caruso name stained with an impossible debt, the Borellis come to collect. 

Luca Dante doesn't just want the money. From the moment he locks eyes with the defiant brunette across the crowded, smoke-choked bar, his dark obsession is ignited. In the underworld, promises are sealed in blood—and Evie is about to discover that some vows are written in

crimson.

Evie squeezed the damp rag in her fist until her knuckles went stark white against the grain of the wood. Marco Caruso was a gambler, a coward, and her older brother, but until tonight, he had never abandoned the Velvet Lantern to face the wolves alone.

The brass-accented clock over the mirrored back bar ticked toward midnight. The jazz pouring from the corner turntable barely masked the heavy silence settling into the velvet-lined booths. It was raining over Chicago, thick sheets of water drumming against the frosted glass front door, blurring the neon sign into a smear of bruised red and amber.

Then the brass bell above the entrance chimed.

A gust of wet, icy wind cut through the warmth of bourbon and beeswax. Two men in tailored black overcoats entered first, flanking the doorway with the practiced stillness of seasoned enforcers. They didn't look at the bar taps or the velvet banquettes; their eyes swept the corners, checking blind spots, before they stepped aside.

Evie’s breath caught in her throat. Her pulse slammed against her ribs like a trapped bird.

The man who walked in between them filled the room without raising his voice or rushing his stride. He pulled off black leather gloves, finger by finger, revealing blunt, scarred knuckles and a signet ring cast in heavy gold bearing the crest of the Borelli family. 

His family called him Luca. To the streets, the docks, and the terrified men who owed him blood, he was Dante. 

Luca Dante Borelli shook the rain from his dark, tailored coat. Beneath it, his three-piece charcoal suit was cut immaculately against wide shoulders and a lean, lethal frame. He had the kind of face that belonged on a renaissance statue carved from cold granite—sharp cheekbones, a strong, unyielding jaw darkened with a day's shadow, and eyes the color of winter smoke that immediately pinned Evie to the spot where she stood behind the mahogany bar.

"We're closed," Evie managed, her voice steady despite the tremor running down her spine. She kept the bar towel in hand, refusing to cower.

Dante didn't answer right away. He moved with a predator’s lazy grace, the soles of his hand-stitched dress shoes clicking softly against the hardwood floor. He stopped directly across from her, resting one palm against the polished counter. His presence was suffocating—smoky vetiver, expensive tobacco, and the ozone tang of rain clinging to him. Up close, the sheer size of him dwarfed her, the dangerous heat rolling off him palpable in the quiet space.

"Dante," she whispered against his skin, the name tasting like smoke and ash.
