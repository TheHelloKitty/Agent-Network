# CRIMSON VOWS

### BLURB
To the blood-soaked streets of the North Ward, he is Dante—a shadow in tailored wool, an enforcer whose name clears alleyways before his footsteps even hit the cobblestones. But behind the heavy oak doors of the Borelli estate, blood demands respect, and there he is simply Luca, the heir born to shoulder an empire built on bone and iron. 

He never intended to look twice at the woman pouring bourbon under the amber glow of The Velvet Lantern. 

Evie Caruso knows the rules of survival in a city ruled by wolves: keep your head down, your apron clean, and never meet the eyes of a man wearing a signet ring. But when an old debt paints a target on her back, the predatory attention of the city’s deadliest predator shifts from the whiskey in his glass to the curve of her throat. 

Luca Dante Borelli doesn’t offer charity. He offers protection—at a price sealed in blood, silk, and a crimson vow that will bind her to his dark world forever.

***

### CHAPTER ONE

The ice in the cut-crystal tumbler had melted down to dull, jagged teeth before he took his first sip.

Evie kept her eyes fixed on the zinc bar top, running the damp rag in slow, circular motions until the grain gleamed under the amber lights of The Velvet Lantern. She didn’t need to look up to know he was watching her. The hair on her arms had been standing on end for the last forty-five minutes, prickling with a raw static that always accompanied the man sitting alone at the corner booth.

To the stray dockworkers and small-time loan sharks who wandered into the Lantern seeking watered-down rye, he was Dante. They whispered the name like an omen, averting their gazes whenever the silhouette of his broad shoulders shifted against the oxblood leather. He was the Borelli syndicate’s hammer—cold, unhurried, and lethal enough to turn the bar dead quiet with nothing more than the scrape of his heavy boots across the floorboards.

Yet tonight, old Marco, who swept the cellar and had known three generations of bosses, had greeted him at the door with a trembling bow and a hushed, *"Signor Luca."*

Luca Dante Borelli. Two names, one beast.

"You're going to strip the lacquer off that wood, little bird."

His voice was a low, gravel-scratched drawl that slid through the jazz humming from the corner speakers. It bypassed Evie’s ears entirely, vibrating straight down into the hollow of her ribs.

Evie stopped her hand, tightening her fingers around the towel. She lifted her chin, meeting the dark, heavy gaze fixed on her from the end of the counter.

He had shed his black cashmere overcoat, draping it over the back of the stool next to him. His shirt was crisp charcoal, unbuttoned twice at the throat to expose golden-olive skin and the sharp lines of collarbones that led down into thick muscle. Rolled-up sleeves revealed forearms mapped with ink—vines of thorns entwined with Roman numerals—and dark veins that stood out against taut skin. He looked like sin carved into flesh, a lethal predator waiting out the clock.
