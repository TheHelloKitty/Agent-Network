When her late uncle’s debts default into the hands of the Borelli syndicate, the city’s most ruthless predator walks through her doors to collect.

### CHAPTER ONE

The air inside The Velvet Lantern tasted of spilled bourbon, clove cigarettes, and the bitter copper tang of impending disaster. 

Behind the mahogany bar, Evie Caruso wiped down the polished wood with mechanical precision. Her knuckles were white, her heart hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird. On stage, a saxophonist blew a low, mournful riff that curled through the amber-lit room, masking the low hum of the patrons—men in sharp suits with guns tucked beneath silk lapels, women in backless dresses laughing too loudly to drown out their own nerves.

The Lantern had always been neutral ground. A place where bosses and foot soldiers alike left their grudges at the velvet-draped entryway. 

Until tonight.

The brass-accented doors swung open. A draft of damp autumn wind swept across the lounge, slicing through the smoke and cutting the room dead. The saxophone died mid-note with a choked squeal. The hushed murmur vanished. Even the clinking of ice against crystal ceased entirely.

Every eye in the room fixed on the man crossing the threshold.

He moved with the predatory grace of an apex killer who knew the world existed solely because he allowed it to. Broad-shouldered, tailored in a charcoal three-piece suit that cost more than Evie made in six months, he wore darkness like a second skin. His ink-black hair was clipped short, framing hard, aristocratic cheekbones, a sharp jawline, and eyes like liquid amber—pale, beautiful, and completely devoid of human mercy.

To his bloodline, to the dynastic monster of an empire built on bone and silence, he was Luca Borelli. 

To the gutter, to the cops, to every terrified bastard between the harbor and the river, he was Dante.

Behind him trailed two heavy-set enforcers, their hands resting lightly inside their coat pockets. But Dante didn’t need an escort. He commanded the oxygen in the room all on his own.

Evie felt a cold drop of sweat slide down the nape of her neck, disappearing into the neckline of her emerald silk blouse. Her uncle Sal had died three weeks ago, coughing up blood in an alleyway, leaving behind a stack of unpaid debts, a mountain of forged receipts, and a single word scrawled in charcoal on his nightstand: *Borelli.*

Now the devil had arrived to balance the ledger.

Dante didn't look at the patrons who flattened themselves against leather booths, practically holding their breath to avoid catching his attention. His gaze locked directly onto Evie. 

A sharp, electric shock skittered down her spine. The intensity of his stare was suffocating—intimate and lethal all at once.

He walked straight toward the bar, his hand-stitched leather shoes making no sound against the Persian rugs. He stopped directly across from her. Up close, he smelled of cedarwood, expensive tobacco, and rain.

"Bourbon," Dante said. 

His voice was a low, dark vibration that hummed through the mahogany wood and settled low in her belly. 

Evie forced her hands not to shake as she reached for the top-shelf crystal decanter. "Neat or on the rocks, Mr. Borelli?"

A razor-thin smile cut across his lips, fleeting and dangerous. "Neat. And in here, you can call me Dante, Little Bird."

Evie’s pulse spiked. *Little Bird.* The nickname rolled off his tongue like velvet and steel. She poured the amber liquid with agonizing care, setting the glass on a linen napkin before sliding it across to him. 

"I don't think you came all the way downtown for a drink, Dante." She kept her chin high, refusing to let him see the terror clawing at her throat. She had survived this city by keeping her spine straight, even when the floor was sinking under her feet.

Dante picked up the glass, swirled the bourbon once, and took a slow sip. His golden-brown eyes never left her face, tracking the rapid flutter of the pulse point at the base of her throat. 

"Sal had poor taste in horses," Dante remarked casually, as if they were discussing the weather. "And worse taste in lenders. Two million dollars, Evie. Cleaned out of our accounts over four months, promised on the profits of this very establishment."
