**TITLE:** Crimson Vows: Shadows of the Velvet Lantern

**BLURB:**
To the underworld, he is Dante—an unyielding enforcer whose name commands silence and blood. To his family, he is Luca Borelli, the loyal son burdened by an empire built on ruthless control. He rules the shadows of the city with cold precision, anchored only by The Velvet Lantern, an opulent lounge that hides illicit deals behind velvet curtains and amber bourbon.

Evie Caruso knows better than to step into Dante’s orbit. After her father’s gambling debts leave her family at the mercy of rival syndicate wolves, Evie takes a job at The Velvet Lantern to secure her independence and protect the only family she has left. She expects brutality; she expects a monster. Instead, she finds a man who watches her from the mezzanine with a dangerous, possessive focus. 

When a violent turf war threatens to incinerate the city, Luca makes Evie an offer disguised as an ultimatum: marry him, take the Borelli name, and gain absolute protection under his mantle. What begins as a strategic alliance turns into an intoxicating clash of wills. Bound by crimson vows, Evie discovers that beneath Dante’s lethal exterior lies Luca—a man whose touch unravels her defenses, and whose dark devotion demands everything she has to give.

***

**CHARACTERS:**

*   **Luca Dante Borelli (Hero):**
    *   *Moniker:* "Luca" to his inner circle and family; "Dante" to the streets and his enemies.
    *   *Role:* Underboss of the Borelli crime family and owner of The Velvet Lantern.
    *   *Traits:* Ruthless, hyper-observant, intensely protective, controlled, and deeply loyal to his bloodline. 
    *   *Arc:* Begins by viewing Evie as a vulnerable liability he feels compelled to shelter; falls completely under her spell, allowing his human side (Luca) to surface only in her presence while wielding Dante's fury to keep her safe.

*   **Evie Caruso (Heroine):**
    *   *Role:* Hostess/managerial lead at The Velvet Lantern; daughter of a disgraced former bookkeeper.
    *   *Traits:* Resilient, sharp-witted, fiercely autonomous, proud, and emotionally intuitive.
    *   *Arc:* Determined never to be traded like collateral by men of the syndicate. She enters the lion's den with her eyes open, refuses to bow to Dante's intimidation, and eventually chooses to claim her place at Luca's side as his equal.



CHAPTER 1



The air inside The Velvet Lantern was thick with the scent of melted beeswax, high-proof rye, and the dangerous perfume of men who settled their debts in blood. Tucked beneath the cracked asphalt of the Meatpacking District, the subterranean lounge was an opulent cage of crimson velvet banquettes, brushed brass, and low-burning amber sconces. It was an oasis of quiet menace, owned and ruled by one man.

Evie Caruso smoothed the front of her tailored black silk trousers, adjusted the neckline of her matching vest, and checked the leather-bound reservation book at the mahogany podium. Her fingers were steady, even if the cold prickle at the base of her skull told her she was being watched. 

She didn't need to look up to know who was standing on the mezzanine. 

Luca Dante Borelli never hid his surveillance. To the city’s concrete veins and the vultures circling the docks, he was Dante—the ruthless Underboss of the Borelli syndicate whose name alone cleared room in crowded alleys. But inside the private books, to his brothers and the few who knew the boy before the violence turned his veins to lead, he was Luca. 

To Evie, he was an overwhelming, suffocating presence who had spent the last six months making her workplace both the safest fortress in the city and the most intoxicating trap she had ever walked into.

"Table four is asking for another round of the private reserve, Evie," Marcus, one of the floor runners, murmured as he passed the podium. "They're getting loud. Salvatore’s crew."

Evie’s gaze snapped toward table four. Three men in cheap, loud suits—soldiers from the rival Marcone outfit—were leaning back, their jackets unbuttoned deliberately to show the holsters tucked against their ribs. They were testing the waters. Her father, before he vanished into the Hudson with missing syndicate ledger pages, had owed money to men exactly like them. 

"I'll handle it," Evie said smoothly, taking a silver tray from the sideboard. 

Before she could take two steps onto the parquet floor, the heavy thud of Italian leather against the iron stairs echoed through the room. The ambient murmur of conversations dropped a fraction of an octave. The brass band near the back lowered their tempo, sliding into a slow, smokey blues riff.

Dante was descending.

He wore a bespoke charcoal suit, the jacket open to reveal a black vest that fit broad, muscular shoulders honed by violence. His dark hair was brushed back, a few stray strands falling near the sharp angle of his jaw, and a faint, silver scar cut through the arch of his left eyebrow. His eyes—a lethal, predatory hazel—never left Evie as he cleared the final step.

He didn't acknowledge the regulars who dipped their heads. He cut straight across the floor, his long stride effortless, stopping until he was so close Evie could smell the cedarwood, tobacco, and clean rain clinging to his coat.

"You're not serving table four, *piccola*," his voice scraped low and rough, meant solely for her ears. 

Evie tilted her chin up, refusing to step backward into the mahogany podium. "It's my floor, Dante. If they want drinks, I bring drinks. That's my job."

A slow, dangerous smirk pulled at the corner of his lips, but his hazel eyes remained ice. "Your job is to manage the house. My job is to keep animals from thinking they can touch what belongs in it."

"I don't belong to the house," she countered softly, her heart hammering against her ribs, traitorously loud. "And I certainly don't belong to you."

Dante leaned in, his large hand coming up to rest on the edge of the podium, effectively caging

Evie didn’t back down. She planted her heels into the floorboards of The Velvet Lantern, refusing to give him the satisfaction of seeing her retreat. Beneath the amber glow of the crystal sconces, the wreckage of the lounge lay scattered—shattered tumblers,

"You look at me like you think you're still untouchable, Evie," Luca Dante murmured, his voice a low, gravelly rasp that scraped straight down her spine. 

He didn't touch her—not yet. But the phantom weight of his presence was an assault on its own. Tall, predatory, broad-shouldered enough to block out the warmth of the chandelier, he looked every inch the butcher the streets called Dante. Only in the private halls of the Borelli estate did they call him Luca. Here, in the belly of the underworld, he was the shadow that took what was owed.

"I’m untouched because you haven’t earned the right," Evie countered, her chin lifting. The silk of her emerald dress fluttered against her thighs as she drew a steady breath, fighting the wild sprint of her pulse. "My father built The Velvet Lantern with Caruso blood, Dante. You might have bought his debts from the council, but the deed doesn't make you king in my house."

A slow, wicked smile touched the corner of his chiseled mouth. It was a predator's grin, laced with cruel amusement and something far darker, simmering just beneath the surface. He reached up, the dark leather of his driving gloves creaking as his knuckles hovered a fraction of an inch from her throat, tracing the rapid beat of her pulse without ever grazing her skin. 

"Your father sold you the moment he put this bar up as collateral to fund his dead-end rackets in the West District," Dante said softly, leaning in until his lips brushed the shell of her ear. The heat of his breath made a violent shiver rip through her. "The Carusos are bankrupt, little bird. The only thing in this room with any true value left... is standing right in front of me."

Evie’s hand twitched toward the silver paring knife she kept hidden under the edge of the polished mahogany bar. Her fingers brushed the cold hilt, but before she could slide it free, Dante’s gloved hand snapped out with lethal speed, pinning her wrist flat against the counter.

The impact was sharp, firm, but calculated. He didn't squeeze hard enough to bruise, but the sheer, effortless dominance of the grip rooted her to the spot.
