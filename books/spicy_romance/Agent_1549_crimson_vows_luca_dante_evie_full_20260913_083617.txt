### Title: Crimson Vows

### Blurb
To the Borelli family, he is Luca—the calculating younger son groomed to balance the ledgers and legitimize a blood-soaked empire. To the underworld of the East District, he is Dante—the ruthless enforcer whose silence is an execution order. One man, two masks, and not a single soul allowed behind either. 

Until Evie Caruso inherits The Velvet Lantern. 

Tucked into the contested border between rival syndicates, Evie’s sultry, brass-and-smoke jazz bar is the only thing standing between her family’s ruin and total annihilation. When a debt she didn't incur brings Dante to her threshold, she refuses to bow. Instead, she strikes a dangerous bargain: his protection inside her walls in exchange for the safe harbor he needs to finish an internal war. 

In the dim glow of The Velvet Lantern, borders blur. Under Dante’s heavy stare and lethal touch, the threat transforms into something entirely intoxicating. But when old blood debts demand payment, Luca must decide whether to tear down his family's throne or let the only woman he burns for become the final sacrifice.

***

### Main Characters

* **Luca Dante Borelli**
  * *Aliases:* "Luca" to his immediate family and high-society contacts; "Dante" to the streets, cartel soldiers, and enforcers.
  * *Role:* Second-born scion and chief strategist/enforcer of the Borelli syndicate. 
  * *Traits:* Coldly methodical, physically lethal, intensely possessive, burdened by the violent legacy he commands. He lives in sharp suits and brass-knuckled shadows, keeping his dual identities strictly segregated until Evie cuts straight through both.

* **Genevieve "Evie" Caruso**
  * *Role:* Proprietor and singer at The Velvet Lantern; daughter of a murdered former Borelli associate.
  * *Traits:* Resilient, sharp-witted, fiercely independent, and fiercely protective of her staff. She wields jazz, sharp liquor, and unmatched resolve to keep the wolves of the city at bay.

### Chapter 1: Blood and Brass

The Velvet Lantern smelled of spilled bourbon, clove cigarettes, and the bitter copper tang of trouble. 

It was half past midnight on a sweltering Friday, and the bassline from the trio on the bandstand vibrated through the floorboards straight into the soles of Evie Caruso’s heels. She stood behind the long mahogany bar, wiping down the polished surface with a bar towel, her gaze sweeping the crowded room with the practiced precision of a hawk. 

Her dress was emerald silk—cut low at the clavicle, clinging to every curve down to her hips, and slit up the left thigh so she could move fast if she needed to. And in this part of the city, south of the rail yards where the streetlights stayed smashed out, a woman always needed to be ready to move.

"More rye for table four, Evie," Marco, her barback, muttered, sliding an empty tray onto the zinc counter. The nineteen-year-old was sweating through his collar, his eyes darting toward the front entrance. "And... we got company at the door."

Evie didn’t look up immediately. She poured two fingers of rye into a lowball glass, set the bottle down with a steady thud, and only then lifted her chin.

Three men had just crossed the threshold beneath the Lantern’s glowing red stained-glass fixture. They weren’t neighborhood regulars, and they certainly weren’t here for the jazz. Heavy wool overcoats despite the summer heat, flat caps pulled low, and the swagger of men who carried lead in their pockets and cruelty in their grins. 

Marcone soldiers. 

Evie’s jaw tightened, but her voice remained like honeyed smoke when she turned to Marco. "Take the tray. Go into the back, lock the stockroom door, and don't come out until I tell you."

"Evie, that’s Dino Rossi," Marco whispered, his voice cracking. "He cracked two ribs off old man Miller last week over fruit cart taxes."

"I know who he is. Back room. Now."

Marco scrambled through the swinging doors into the kitchen.
