### Title: Crimson Vows

### Blurb
To the Borelli syndicate, he is Luca—the dutiful firstborn heir bound by tradition and blood. To the ruthless criminal underworld of the North End, he is Dante—an unyielding enforcer whose whisper clears streets and silences rival bosses. 

Evie Caruso wants nothing to do with either world. All she wants is to keep The Velvet Lantern, her late father’s subterranean jazz lounge, alive and independent. But when an old blood debt surfaces bearing her father’s signature, the Caruso family table is shattered, leaving Evie cornered. The Borelli patriarch demands settlement, and the price is absolute: an arranged marriage to bind the territories once and for all.

Luca Dante steps into the amber glow of The Velvet Lantern not to negotiate, but to claim what the contract demands. Yet Evie refuses to be broken by a ring or a ruthless name. Behind heavy oak doors and beneath the guise of cold alliance, sharp wit and simmering tension boil over into an all-consuming fire. When betrayals from within the Borelli ranks threaten to tear their fragile truce apart, Dante will burn the city to protect his bride, while Luca must surrender his hardened heart to the only woman bold enough to claim it.

---

### Main Characters

* **Luca Dante Borelli**
  * *Aliases:* "Luca" to his immediate family and inner circle; "Dante" to the streets, business rivals, and those who fear him.
  * *Role:* Heir and primary enforcer of the Borelli syndicate. 
  * *Traits:* Imposing, calculating, fiercely protective, intensely disciplined until pushed to his limits. Possesses a dangerous dual nature—the refined strategist and the lethal predator.

* **Evie Caruso**
  * *Role:* Proprietress of The Velvet Lantern jazz lounge; sole surviving heiress to the faded Caruso influence.
  * *Traits:* Resilient, sharp-tongued, fiercely loyal to her staff and independence. Refuses to cower in the face of cartel dominance, meeting Dante’s intimidating presence with defiant grace.

#### Supporting Characters
* **Salvatore Borelli:** Patriarch of the Borelli family; Luca’s demanding father who prioritizes dynasty over sentiment.
* **Marco "Vane" Caruso:** Evie’s wayward cousin whose reckless gambling and secret deals triggered the syndicate's takeover.
* **Gia Moretti:** Evie’s head bartender, confidante, and protective right hand at The Velvet Lantern.
* **Silvio Rossi:** Underboss allied with Borelli rivals, harboring ambitions to usurp the North End territory.

CHAPTER 1



The brass lamps of The Velvet Lantern cast pools of melted amber across the lacquered mahogany bar. The scent of sweet bourbon, stale clove cigarettes, and rain hung heavy in the air, wrapping the lounge in the tired luxury that had kept the Caruso name alive long after its bloodline lost its teeth.

Evie Caruso wiped down the bar with steady, practiced circles. Her black silk blouse clung to the curve of her spine, the cuffs turned back to reveal bruised wrists—not from violence, but from hauling crates of top-shelf liquor that cost twice what she took in each night. 

"Last call came and went twenty minutes ago, Evie," Gia said, setting an empty bucket on the stainless-steel prep station. Her sharp dark eyes lingered on the street entrance, where sheet rain battered the leaded-glass door. "Lock up before the vultures start circling."

"Five more minutes," Evie murmured without looking up. Her auburn hair slipped from its loose pin, brushing the collar of her shirt. "Marco swore he’d drop off the manifest from the docks. If I don't balance the books before dawn, the tax assessors will crawl down my throat."

"Marco is a degenerate gambler who’s going to drag you into an unmarked grave," Gia snapped, though her tone softened with pity. "He hasn't brought anything through those doors except bad news in six months."

Before Evie could answer, the frosted glass panel rattled violently. 

The brass handle jerked down. The heavy oak door burst inward on its hinges with a sickening crack, letting in a gust of cold, rain-soaked air that flickered every candle in the room.

Marco stumbled across the threshold. 

He didn't walk; he fell, catching himself against the corner of a high-backed burgundy booth. His trench coat was soaked black, but the crimson blooming across the shoulder of his pale linen shirt wasn't rain. It was thick, arterial, and dripping steadily onto the herringbone floor.

"Evie," he wheezed, his face gray beneath the yellow lamps. He clutched his side, fingers slick with red. "Lock... lock the back. God, Evie, lock the fucking door."

"Marco!" Evie dropped the rag, vaulting the counter without hesitation. The sting of panic tasted like pennies on her tongue. She grabbed him beneath the arm, her boots sliding slightly on his wet trail. "What did you do? Who did this?"

"He's right behind me," Marco gasped, his pupils blown wide, trembling so violently his teeth clicked. "I didn't know the crates were his. I swear to Christ, I thought they were Rossi’s—"

"Shut up," Evie hissed, dragging his dead weight toward the back hallway that led to the office and the fire escape. "Gia, call Dr. Keller. Now."

"Too late for the doctor, *piccola*."

The voice didn't rise above a low, gravel baritone, but it froze the marrow in Evie’s bones. 

The silhouette framed in the open doorway was tall, broad-shouldered, and entirely at ease amidst the violence he trailed. He stepped out of the pouring dark and into the lantern light, shaking off an umbrella before leaning it casually against the brass coat stand. 

Luca Dante Borelli.

To the high-society boardrooms on the hill, he was Luca—the refined heir to a billion-dollar shipping and steel syndicate, educated in Switzerland, impeccably tailored, moving through galas with aristocratic precision. 

To the docks, the back alleys, and the men who bled in the gutters, he was Dante. The executioner. The shadow that walked with a blade and never left a witness alive unless they were broken beyond repair.

Tonight, the tailored facade and the butcher coexisted in lethal harmony.

hair was swept back, damp strands falling over a forehead marked by sharp, aristocratic bone structure. Beneath heavy, brooding brows, his amber-hazel eyes caught the amber sconces of the Velvet Lantern, glowing with the predatory intelligence of a wolf that had cornered its prey in its own den.

Evie didn’t drop Marco, but her fingers dug into the fabric of the boy’s soaked jacket until her knuckles turned white. 

"Get out, Borelli," Evie said. Her voice didn't waver, though her heart pounded against her ribs like a trapped bird. "You don't spill blood in my house."

Luca Dante closed the heavy oak door behind him. The brass latch clicked into place with a sound as sharp and definitive as a hammer cocking. He stepped forward, his polished leather boots making no sound on the wet parquet. Behind him, two shadows remained outside, silhouetted against the frosted glass of the entrance. 

"Your house, Genevieve?" His voice rolled over her skin, dark and velvety, thick with an accent born of Sicilian steel wrapped in private-school silk. "Your father built this speakeasy on Borelli money. Borelli blood bought the timber. Borelli permits kept the city inspectors blind." 

He stopped five feet from them. Up close, the sheer size of him dwarfed the hallway. The scent of him invaded her senses—rainwater, imported tobacco, and the cold, metallic edge of fresh gun smoke. 

"Don't call me Genevieve," she snapped, stepping slightly in front of Marco, though the dead-weight boy was already slipping into shock, whimpering behind her boots. 

Luca’s gaze drifted from Evie’s fierce green eyes down the line of her throat, lingering on the heavy rise and fall of her chest beneath her black silk vest and rolled-up white sleeves. A slow, infuriating smirk touched the corner of his chiseled mouth. 

"A tiger guarding a rat," Luca murmured, his voice dipping low, intimate in a way that made a sudden, betraying flare of heat ripple low in Evie's belly. He reached into his coat. 

Evie’s hand darted beneath the polished mahogany bar, her fingers brushing the cold grip of the snub-nosed .38 she kept taped beneath the counter.

Luca didn't flinch. He didn't even pause. With terrifying grace, he drew a monogrammed silk handkerchief from his pocket and wiped a microscopic fleck of scarlet from his left cufflink. 

"Draw that toy, *cara*, and I will make you swallow it," he said evenly, not even looking at her hand. "Step aside. Marco took three crates of uncut diamonds off the Borelli freight at pier nine. He knows the penalty."

"I was paid! The Rossi capo—he set me up!" Marco sobbed, crawling toward Evie's boots, smearing crimson across the floorboards. "Miss Caruso, please, for God's sake—"

"Silence," Luca commanded. 

The word was barely more than an exhale, but Marco choked on his own breath, instantly mute, trembling so hard his forehead knocked against the floor. 

Luca took two slow, measured strides forward, closing the distance between himself and Evie until barely a breath of air separated them. He towered over her, his broad shoulders blocking out the warm light of the bar, casting her entirely in his shadow. The heat radiating off his massive frame was suffocating, intoxicating, and lethal. 

"You're bleeding on my floor, Borelli," Evie whispered, tilting her chin up to meet his golden, soulless eyes. "You want him? You carry him outside. The Velvet Lantern is neutral ground. Your father signed that accord with mine ten years ago."

Luca looked down at her. His eyes darkened, pupils expanding until the amber burned like a thin ring around pure midnight. Very slowly, he raised one leather-gloved hand and rested two fingers against the hollow of her throat. 

Her pulse fluttered wild and erratic against his knuckles. He felt it. She knew he felt it.

"My father is rotting in a mausoleum, Evie," Luca rasped, stepping so close his chest brushed her crossed arms. "And I am not my father."

"You're Dante," she whispered, the street name slipping out, laced with venom and an involuntary shudder. 

His lips parted slightly, a dark, wicked gleam flashing across his face as he absorbed the name on her tongue. "To the gutter, yes. But to you..." His thumb dragged along the delicate curve of her jaw, firm enough to tilt her face higher, forcing her to see the lethal promise carved into every line of his features. "...to you, I am whatever I choose to be."

"Back off, Luca."

He smiled, a slash of white teeth in the dim amber gloom. "You owe me forty thousand dollars for the shipment your little stray ruined with sea water."

"I don't owe you a dime."

"Then you pay in collateral." Luca's hand dropped from her jaw, his heavy gaze raking down her form, slow and unapologetic, searing her skin right through her clothes. "Give me the boy, and I forget you hid a thief under your skirt."

"He's a kid," Evie said between clenched teeth. "He made a mistake."

"He stole from the Borelli family," Luca's voice hardened, the refined heir vanishing entirely behind the ruthless mobster. "There are no mistakes. Only consequences."

He didn't wait for her permission. Moving with sudden, terrifying speed, Luca reached past her, his massive hand fisting in the collar of Marco's sodden coat. He hoisted the grown man off the floor with one arm as if he weighed nothing, turning toward the door. 

"Luca, stop!" Evie lunged forward, grabbing his forearm. 

The muscle beneath his coat was solid as iron. Luca paused, glancing down at her small hand gripping his sleeve, then slowly raised his eyes back to her face. The tension between them snapped taut, humming with danger, power, and an electric undercurrent that made her breath catch in her throat.

"You touch me again, Evie," he warned softly, his voice dropping into a register that vibrated straight to her core, "and you come with him."
