### Title
**Crimson Vows**

---

### Blurb
To the criminal underworld of New York, he is Dante—an unyielding enforcer with blood on his hands and ice in his veins. To the powerful Borelli syndicate, he is Luca, the loyal scion bound to preserve his family’s empire at any cost. Luca Dante Borelli moves between shadow and blood without hesitation, until his territory intersects with The Velvet Lantern.

Evie Caruso thought she had scrubbed herself clean of mafia ties. Managing The Velvet Lantern—a moody, velvet-draped cocktail lounge nestled in the heart of the city—is supposed to be her sanctuary. But her estranged father left behind a crushing debt, and the Borellis have come to collect.

Instead of shuttering her haven, Luca offers an ultimatum: Evie keeps her bar and her life, provided she plays the part of his devoted fiancée to solidify an alliance within the syndicate. Behind closed doors, their animosity catches fire, turning calculated proximity into dark, consuming obsession. As rival factions close in and loyalties shatter, Evie must decide if surrendering to the monster of the streets will destroy her—or if taking his crimson vow is the only way they both survive.

---

### Characters

*   **Luca Dante Borelli**
    *   *Identity:* Eldest enforcer and heir apparent of the Borelli crime family. Called "Luca" by blood relatives and inner circle; feared on the streets strictly as "Dante."
    *   *Traits:* Ruthless, tactical, possessive, fiercely protective. Carries the physical and psychological scars of syndicate wars.
    *   *Goal:* Secure his family's dominion against an encroaching cartel while keeping Evie bound securely beneath his protection.

*   **Evie Caruso**
    *   *Identity:* Owner-manager of The Velvet Lantern; daughter of a disgraced former bookkeeper for the syndicate.
    *   *Traits:* Defiant, resourceful, observant, deeply loyal to her staff and independent life.
    *   *Goal:* Protect The Velvet Lantern, escape her family's blood debt, and guard her heart against a man capable of setting her world ablaze.

*   **Supporting Cast:**
    *   *Salvatore Borelli:* The aging Don and Luca’s father, who demands unquestioned obedience to the bloodline.
    *   *Matteo:* Luca's trusted right-hand soldier in street operations.
    *   *Rochelle:* The Velvet Lantern’s head bartender and Evie’s fiercely loyal confidante.

CHAPTER 1



The mist hanging over St. Jude’s Alley smelled of sulfur, river damp, and copper. 

Luca Dante Borelli did not hurry his footsteps. He moved with the quiet, predatory cadence of a man who owned the dark and every soul unfortunate enough to bleed in it. Behind him, Matteo kept three paces back, hands buried in the pockets of his heavy wool coat, eyes scanning the fire escapes above.

Ahead, three men were backed against the brickwork of a dead-end loading bay. They wore the colors of the Marcone splinter faction—scavengers pushing south of the canal, testing the perimeter of Borelli ground. 

"You're out of your lane, boys," Matteo said, his voice flat.

The man in the center spat blood onto the wet cobblestones. "This city doesn't belong to old men in stone mansions anymore. Salvatore's half in the grave."

Luca didn't blink. The family called him Luca; the baptismal font had received him under that name, and in the quiet halls of the Borelli estate, it was spoken with deference to the bloodline. But here, where the streetlights hummed with dying filaments and the damp soaked through leather soles, they called him Dante. Because entering his crosshairs meant stepping directly into the inferno.

Luca closed the distance in three measured strides. The spokesman lunged, flashing a serrated trench knife, but Luca moved with lethal economy. He caught the man’s wrist, twisting hard until the bone cracked with the wet snap of green wood. Before the scream could fully tear from the man's throat, Luca slammed him face-first into the soot-stained brick, driving his forearm against the back of his neck.

"Salvatore sleeps in silk," Luca murmured, his voice a low, gravelly rasp against the man's ear. "I sleep in the dirt with dogs like you. Remember who collects the toll."

He drew his sidearm with his free hand, pressing the cold steel of the barrel directly behind the man’s ear. The other two men froze, their hands shooting into the air, their chests heaving with raw terror.

"Drop your pieces," Matteo ordered from behind, his own gun leveled with clinical ease.

Metal clattered against wet stone.

Luca released the broken man, letting him slide into the filth at his feet. He wiped a stray drop of blood from his cheek with the back of his leather glove, his dark eyes sweeping over the remaining two. "Tell Marcone that every foot of ground past the canal carries a tax payable in bone. If I see your faces east of Mercer Street again, Matteo won't even bother waking me up before he buries you."

"Understood," one choked out, dragging his broken companion upright. "We're gone. We're gone."

Luca watched them scramble into the fog, dragging their heels, leaving smears of red across the flagstones. He holstered his piece and checked his watch. Eleven forty-five.

"Messy," Matteo commented, nudging one of the discarded revolvers into a storm drain with the toe of his boot.

"Messy sends a cleaner message," Luca replied, rolling his broad shoulders beneath his tailored charcoal coat. The adrenaline hummed in his veins, but it did not settle the quiet, relentless ache in his chest—the instinct that had been pulling his gaze toward the eastern district all night. Toward the glow of frosted glass and aged brass.

"Where now, Dante?" Matteo asked. "The Don expects us back at the compound by one. The council meets at dawn."

"Go ahead to the estate," Luca said, his gaze fixed down the alleyway where the street opened toward the riverfront. "Tell my father the southern border is secure. I have personal business to inspect."

Matteo paused, studying his capo’s stone-carved profile under the flickers of a failing streetlamp. A knowing, dangerous smirk touched the corner of the soldier’s mouth, but he knew better than to push it. "The girl at the taproom again?"

"Watch your tongue, Matteo," Luca said softly. The tone carried no shout, no heat, just the cold weight of an iron vault swinging shut. "Before I give it to the stray dogs on Mercer."

Matteo bowed his head slightly, stepping back into the shadow of the idling town car. "Understood, boss. Be careful. Marcone's rats are bold tonight."

Luca didn’t answer. He turned on his heel and cut through the mist, leaving the stench of blood and cordite behind him. 

Ten minutes later, the gritty grime of the warehouse docks gave way to the narrow, cobblestone arteries of the riverfront quarter. Tucked into the bend of an alley lined with weeping brick stood a modest façade of black lacquer and stained glass, crowned by a wrought-iron sign that creaked softly in the damp wind: *The Velvet Lantern.*

To the city, it was just another quiet dive where sailors and off-duty dockworkers drowned their miseries in cheap gin and lukewarm draft. To Luca Dante Borelli, it was the only sanctuary in a city made of ash and crossfire.

He pushed the heavy mahogany door open. The low brass bell above gave a solitary, muffled chime. 

Inside, the world smelled of clove tobacco, aged cedar, spilled rye, and a delicate undercurrent that made Luca’s chest tighten—vanilla and bruised gardenias. The bar was nearly empty, the last few drunkards huddled in the rear booths beneath amber sconces, their voices muted against the low murmur of a jazz record spinning on a dusty phonograph in the corner.

Behind the long, copper-topped counter stood Evie Caruso.

She was wiping down the polished surface with a white rag, her slender back turned to the door. Her dark hair was pinned up loosely, several glossy strands escaping to brush the pale, graceful curve of her nape. She wore a simple burgundy slip of a blouse beneath a dark apron, the soft linen hugging the dip of her waist and the swell of her hips. 

Luca didn't make a sound as he crossed the floor, but the hair on the back of Evie’s neck rose. She froze, then slowly turned.

When her hazel eyes met his, the breath hitched in her throat, visible in the rapid rise of her chest. 

"You're late, Dante," she murmured, her voice like velvet dragged over gravel, low and steady despite the slight tremor in her hands. 

"Traffic on the bridge," Luca replied smoothly. He took his usual stool at the far corner of the bar, the shadowy nook near the brick pillar where he could watch both exits without taking his eyes off her. 

He shrugged out of his heavy charcoal overcoat, draping it over the adjacent barstool. Underneath, his black dress shirt strained against the broad expanse of his chest and shoulders, the sleeves rolled cleanly to the forearms, revealing veins like twisted rope and the faint, jagged scars of a life built on slaughter.

Evie reached under the counter and pulled down a bottle of amber whiskey—the good stuff, the bottle she kept hidden behind the sour mash solely for him. She set a heavy cut-crystal tumbler before him and poured two fingers without asking.

"Rough night?" she asked softly.

Luca picked up the glass. As he lifted it, the amber light caught the pale, clean skin of his knuckles. He had scrubbed the blood off with a damp rag in the alley, but there was still a faint red line beneath the rim of his thumb nail

"Whose was it?" Evie asked softly. Her fingers were cool against the feverish heat radiating from his flesh.

Her gaze flicked up, meeting his. Her hazel eyes were vast in the dim amber glow of the hanging pendants, clear and impossibly defiant. Any other woman in North Boston would have crossed herself, panicked, or begged for mercy. "I don't like blood on my floor, Luca," she whispered, leaning in just enough that the heat of her breath brushed his bruised cheek.

The streets called him Dante—a specter of violence, a demon in an Italian wool suit who executed his father’s will without hesitation. But when she murmured *Luca*, the syllable fell from her lips like an invocation, stripping away the ruthless mantle until he was merely a man burning alive for her.

"Then it's a good thing I kept it all on my sleeves," he murmured, his gaze dropping to the plush curve of her mouth. 

Evie pressed the warm cloth to the cut across his knuckles. A sharp sting flared, but Luca didn't so much as twitch. He only stared at her, drinking in the flushed warmth rising along her throat. She applied pressure with a deliberate, firm stroke, tending to the wound with the clinical efficiency of someone who had grown up in the shadows of the syndicate, yet with an intimacy that made the air between them razor-thin.

"You're reckless," she chided under her breath, tossing the stained cloth into a copper basin behind her. She reached beneath the counter and brought out a bottle of iodine and a clean roll of linen tape. "Showing up here looking like a butcher while the Marcone crew is sniffing around every corner of the North End."

"Let them sniff," Luca said, his fingers curling slightly around the edge of her waist apron, trapping a fold of the dark canvas between his fingers. He pulled, just an inch, coaxing her to step closer into his space. "They won't cross this threshold. They know who owns the territory."

"You don't own me, Dante," she warned, using the street name like a shield, though the quickening flutter of her pulse in her throat betrayed her.

"Never said I did, *tesoro*," Luca growled softly, his thumb rubbing the heavy linen against the swell of her hip. "If I owned you, you wouldn't be standing on the other side of this mahogany. You'd be locked behind my door, where nobody else gets to breathe your air."

Evie froze, the uncapped bottle of iodine hovering inches from his battered hand. A shiver rippled through her, unmistakable and primal. She didn't retreat. Instead, she leaned closer over the bar, her knuckles resting flat on the mahogany just an inch from his thighs, meeting his predatory stare head-on.

"Is that a threat, Borelli?"

"It’s a promise I’m barely keeping to myself," he admitted, his jaw tightening as the dark, possessive hunger clawed at his chest.
