Evie knew the rules because she survived by them.

Chapter Two

The brass-studded oak door swung open, slicing the haze with an intake of cold, rain-soaked air that made the amber sconces flicker along the walls. 

The low hum of conversation across the Velvet Lantern died instantly.

Evie didn’t need to look up to know who had crossed the threshold, but her body betrayed her anyway. The hairs on the nape of her neck stood on end, a sharp, electric shiver traveling down her spine. Men like Enzo survived in the gutters by smelling danger before it struck, and right now Enzo went utterly still, his knuckles whitening around his tumbler of rye before he hastily slid off his stool and vanished into the shadows of the rear booths.

Luca Dante Borelli walked in like he owned the ground beneath everyone’s feet—and in this city, he nearly did.

To the family, to the inner bloodline of the Borelli syndicate who whispered behind high estate gates, he was Luca. But to the streets, to the men who bled out in wet alleys and the judges who took kickbacks in dark cars, he was Dante. A specter in a bespoke charcoal wool coat, broad-shouldered and lethal, carrying the kind of silence that preceded an execution.

Rain glistened on the tailored wool over his shoulders as he unbuttoned it, revealing the crisp black vest and silk tie beneath. His dark hair was swept back, damp from the storm outside, highlighting the angular slash of his cheekbones and the hard, uncompromising line of his jaw. He moved with a predator’s easy grace, cutting a path through the crowded lounge without brushing a single shoulder. People simply parted for him, desperate not to draw his obsidian gaze.

He didn't look at any of them. His eyes were locked entirely on Evie.

Her pulse beat a frantic, traitorous rhythm against her ribs, but she kept her hands steady on the bar rag, smoothing it over the black mahogany until he closed the distance. The scent of him hit her first—expensive cedarwood, cold rain, Italian leather, and the faint, bitter metallic tang of gunpowder that lingered like a signature.

Dante pulled out the leather-topped barstool directly across from her and sat. He didn't lean forward. He simply commanded the space, his broad frame casting a heavy shadow across the polished wood.

"Dante," Evie said, her voice smooth and devoid of the tremor vibrating in her chest. Neutral ground demanded neutrality, even when the devil himself took a seat at her counter.

"Caruso," he replied, his voice a low, gravel-rich baritone that scraped over her nerves like velvet over raw wire. 

He didn't reach for a menu, and she didn't bother asking. She turned to the top shelf, reaching for the crystal decanter filled with twenty-year-old reserve Scotch. When she set the heavy cut-glass tumbler down, the liquid caught the dim amber light like liquid fire. 

She poured two fingers, neat. 

"You're late tonight," she murmured, setting the bottle down with a soft *clink*.
